**Short answer:** Google Ads captures homeowners who are already searching for "custom closets near me," so the intent is high but clicks are expensive and you compete with franchises. Facebook and Instagram ads reach homeowners before they search, using photos of your work, so each lead costs less but needs filtering. With a budget under about $2,000 a month, most independent closet companies get more booked estimates starting on Meta with a pre-qualifying form, then adding Google once the calendar is steady.

## The core difference: intent vs discovery

**Google** shows your ad when someone types a search. That person has a need right now. You pay for the click, and in home improvement categories those clicks are often among the most expensive in local advertising.

**Facebook and Instagram** show your ad to people in your area who match a profile, while they scroll. Most weren't looking for a closet five seconds ago. A great before and after photo is what makes them stop. You create the demand instead of catching it.

| | Google Ads | Facebook and Instagram |
|---|---|---|
| Homeowner intent | High: already searching | Medium: inspired by your work |
| Cost per click | High in home improvement | Lower |
| Competition | Franchises with big budgets | Fewer closet companies doing it well |
| What sells | Your offer and reviews | Your photos and videos |
| Needs filtering | Some | Yes, budget and timeline questions |
| Time to first leads | Days | Days |

## Why closets are a visual sale

A custom closet is something people want once they see it. Very few homeowners wake up and search for "walk-in closet with island and LED lighting." They see one, they picture their own messy closet and then they want it. That's why Meta often outperforms Google for this category on a small budget: the format matches how the product gets sold.

## When Google should come first

- You're in a dense metro where many people search for "custom closets" every day.
- Your budget is above $2,000 a month and you can afford a few weeks of testing.
- You have strong Google reviews and a fast, clear website.
- You want to catch move-in season and new homeowners who are actively shopping.

Also look at **Google Local Services Ads** (the ones with the "Google Guaranteed" badge). You pay per lead instead of per click, and they show at the very top of the results. They're worth testing once your reviews are solid.

## When Facebook and Instagram should come first

- Your budget is $900 to $2,000 a month and you need booked estimates, not just clicks.
- You have photos or videos of your own installs.
- You're willing to ask qualifying questions so you don't waste time on browsers.
- Your area has a big Spanish-speaking market: you can run the same campaign in Spanish and reach buyers your competitors ignore.

## The plan that works for most independent companies

1. **Months 1 to 2:** Meta ads with an instant form asking budget, timeline and zip code. Reply within five minutes. Measure cost per booked estimate.
2. **Month 3:** Retarget people who watched your videos or visited your site. They're your warmest audience and the cheapest to convert.
3. **Once the calendar is steady:** add Google Local Services Ads or a tight Google Search campaign on high-intent terms in your best zip codes.

## Frequently asked questions

?? Is Google Ads or Facebook Ads better for a custom closet business?
Google wins on intent, Facebook and Instagram win on cost and on showing your work. Under about $2,000 a month, start with Meta and a pre-qualifying form; add Google when you can fund both.

?? Why are Google clicks so expensive for closets?
Home improvement is one of the most competitive local categories, and franchises bid aggressively on the same searches. You pay for every click, including people who only want a price.

?? Can I run both at the same time?
Yes, and long term you should. Just don't split a small budget in two; each channel needs enough spend to produce data. Get one working, then add the other.

?? Do Facebook ads work for high-ticket closets?
Yes, when the ad shows your real work and the form filters by budget and timeline. A $6,000 closet sells on trust and visuals, which is exactly what Facebook and Instagram are good at.
